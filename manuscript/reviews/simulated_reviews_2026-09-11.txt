# Peer Review — Reviewer A (Methods & Statistics)

**Manuscript:** *Coordinate-aware peak-to-trough estimation from deterministic 2bRAD anchors* (Microbiome)

## 1. Summary

The paper presents sk2bGrow, a peak-to-trough ratio (PTR) estimator that replaces the sketch-and-sorted-rank design of Pilea with (i) motif-defined Type IIB restriction-enzyme anchors whose genome coordinates are known a priori and (ii) a coordinate-aware windowed "V-fit" with per-enzyme inverse-variance fusion and a Cochran's-Q-based quality control. On the Zheng *E. coli* growth-rate panel it reports usable PTR estimates at 1–2× depth (r = 0.981 at 1×, n = 16) where Pilea's shipped gates return nothing, attributes the gain to the estimator × landmark-set interaction via a controlled 2 × 2, recommends a 4–8-enzyme panel over the full 16, and adds real-metagenome concordance with Pilea on three fecal samples, a MAG-scale QC/cost study, and a process-level finding that exact deduplication destroys the 2bRAD PTR signal. The paper is unusually candid about its own claim boundaries, which I credit; my concerns are about statistical uncertainty, estimator stability across analysis configurations, and a small number of claims that the authors' own records do not fully support.

## 2. Overall assessment

**Recommendation: major revision.**

Scores (1–5): **novelty 4 · technical rigor 3 · biological/clinical significance 3 · clarity 4 · reproducibility 4.**

The estimator design and the 2 × 2 attribution are genuinely novel and the reporting of bias/slope beside correlation is exemplary — but the flagship low-coverage numbers are unstable across analysis harnesses in a way that dwarfs several reported effects, no uncertainty quantification is provided anywhere, and at least one headline cost fix (the containment screen) is presented one-sidedly relative to the authors' own internal records. None of these is fatal; all are addressable.

## 3. Major comments

**M1. The headline 0.5× result is harness-unstable to a degree that dwarfs most effects the paper reports.** §2.1/Table 2 claims r = 0.913 at 0.5× (arm A, `tables/table2_ecoli_accuracy.tsv`; reproducible from `data/results_raw.tsv`, slope 0.615). The F1 attribution harness — on the same Zheng grid, same estimator family — gives the panel arm r = 0.568 at 0.5× (`data/f1_sketch/F1_results.tsv`, arm A_k16; the k-matched panel arms span 0.454–0.568). At 1× the gap is 0.075 r (0.981 committed vs 0.906 in F1). The manuscript attributes this to a parity-flag asymmetry ("A with `--windows`, E without… a symmetric rerun remains a check, not a result", §4) and forbids cross-harness comparison — but this is precisely the problem: the paper's central low-coverage claim lives in the depth band where the result swings by **0.34 r depending on an undocumented run flag**. The reader cannot tell which configuration produced Table 2, or whether the 0.913 would survive the symmetric harness. *Fix:* run the symmetric A/E harness rerun (the authors themselves call it "a check, not a result" — make it a result); state which configuration produces each committed number; if 0.913 at 0.5× is configuration-dependent, say so in the Abstract, not only in §4.

**M2. The containment-screen claim is one-sided, and the "21.1×" figure is unverifiable from the repository.** §7c states the M4 containment pre-screen "clears the absent-genome false-positive floor (4,048 of 4,700 … receive nonzero counts unrestricted; zero with screen), returning 21.1× at 90% selection stringency", and concludes "the fixes are measured rather than projected." The false-positive census is verified in the authors' own records (`data/c5_review/REVIEW.md`, §5: 4,048/4,700 synthetic genomes nonzero unrestricted, 0 with screen). But the same records contain a C5-scale A/B of the screen that the manuscript never mentions: wall-clock 14:33 unrestricted vs 14:39 screened — i.e. **no speedup (~1.0×)** in the measured scenario (REVIEW §4d) — and the screen **dropped 65 of 522 real MAGs entirely** (12.5%), because low-abundance real genomes fail the `--screen-min-frac` threshold (REVIEW §4d: "丢失的正是低覆盖 MAG… 对 PTR 场景不可接受"; partially reframed in §4e but the loss stands). I searched the repository for the 21.1× figure and it is **unverifiable from repo** — no data file contains it. The sentence "the fixes are measured rather than projected" is therefore not supported for the screen arm as written. *Fix:* report the true-positive-loss side alongside the false-positive census, state that the measured C5 A/B shows no speedup at ~90% presence rate, and either source or delete the 21.1× figure (if it is a model-based projection for GTDB-scale presence rates, label it as such).

**M3. No uncertainty quantification anywhere; the central "interaction" claim is never subjected to any test.** Every headline number in the paper — r = 0.981 (n = 16), slope 0.78, "the two landmark sources are indistinguishable at ≥ 1×", "r peaks at k = 8" — is a point estimate with no confidence interval, no Fisher-z comparison, and no definition of the noise band invoked by "within noise" (§4). The 2 × 2 attribution is the paper's core contribution ("the 2 × 2 is what licenses any statement of the form 'the gain comes from X'", Methods §4), yet the interaction is never tested as an interaction — it is read off cell differences by eye. For scale: at 1× the matched-density arms differ by ≤0.03 r (A_k16 0.906 vs E_s104 0.909, F1_results.tsv), and with n = 16 a Fisher-z test has SE ≈ √(2/13) ≈ 0.39 in z units, so "indistinguishable" is plausible but the paper gives the reader no way to know the claim has been quantified rather than eyeballed. Similarly "r peaks at k = 8" (Table 7) is a maximum over five correlated panel sizes on the same 16-media grid, with no interval. *Fix:* (a) define and apply an explicit equivalence/noise criterion (e.g. Fisher-z CI or bootstrap over media) wherever "within noise" or "indistinguishable" appears; (b) report bootstrap CIs for r, slope and RMSE in Tables 2 and 7; (c) where per-sample estimate pairs exist (F1, §7a), compare paired estimates rather than correlations of 16 points.

**M4. The slope silently changes reference mid-paper — the exact circularity hazard Methods §3.1 warns about.** The Discussion states: "the mild over-estimation at depth (slope 1.03 at 5× and 1.07 at 10×, against 0.62 at 0.5× and 0.78 at 1×)". But Methods §5 defines slope as the OLS slope of *ŷ* on **λ**, and Table 2 reports exactly that: 0.920 at 5×, 0.951 at 10× (`tables/table2_ecoli_accuracy.tsv`). I recomputed both from `data/results_raw.tsv`: slope on λ = 0.9197/0.9506; slope on predicted log₂PTR = 1.0298/1.0740. So the Discussion's 1.03/1.07 are slopes against the *predicted* log₂PTR — the quantity Methods §3.1 itself flags as "not fully independent of sequencing" because C was derived in part from marker-frequency analysis. *Fix:* adopt one slope reference throughout. If slopes against predicted log₂PTR are quoted, label them as such and restate the circularity caveat at that sentence, not twenty pages earlier.

**M5. The direction of Cochran's Q's dependence bias appears to be misstated.** Methods §1.6 ("Known gap") and the Discussion state that because Bsp24I's site set is contained in CjePI's, "Q is mildly anti-conservative." With *positively dependent* strata, the weighted scatter of estimates around the fused mean is *smaller* than under independence, so Q is stochastically **deflated** and the test loses power — i.e. conservative for detecting heterogeneity, not anti-conservative. The degrees-of-freedom argument points the same way (χ² on 15 df where ~14 effective strata exist gives larger p-values). Since Q is simultaneously the QC gate and the trigger for the DerSimonian–Laird re-weighting, its calibration is not a footnote: a conservative Q means the fused fixed-effect SE is used exactly when heterogeneity is present but undetected. *Fix:* justify the direction (e.g. simulate Q under the measured Bsp24I–CjePI correlation structure) or correct the statement, and note the implication for when the DL escalation fires.

**M6. F2 boundary claims overstate the underlying data.** §5 states "(at ≥ 1× all cells give r ≥ 0.78; 2×/5× give r ≥ 0.95/0.98)". Checking `data/f2_gc_sweep/F2_accuracy.tsv`, at 1× four cells fall below 0.78, all on the two low-GC genomes: g00 A_k2 r = 0.671, E_s209 0.640, E_s250 0.724, and g01 A_k16 0.775 (borderline). The quoted 0.78 floor is exactly g01's A_k16 value rounded — it reads as if cherry-picked. If the parenthetical is meant to apply *within* the stated operating range (GC ≳ 30%), the true floor at 1× is 0.85 (g03 A_k8) and should be stated as such. Second, "k8 and k16 accuracy differ by < 0.1 r at GC ≥ 33% at 0.5×" is violated at *Staphylococcus equorum* (33.0% GC): A_k8 r = 0.698 vs A_k16 0.822, Δ = 0.124. Third, the GC ≤ 30% boundary is measured on exactly two genomes, both 0.65 Mb *Buchnera* — the paper flags the genome-size confound, but then the stated operating range must be written as "GC ≳ 30% **and genome ≳ 1.6 Mb**", since the design cannot separate the two. Finally, boundary cells carry n = 5–8 truth replicates with up to 3 failed cells (e.g. g01 A_k2 0.5×: n = 7, 1 failed); an r on n = 5 has a confidence interval wider than the claimed boundary. *Fix:* correct the two numeric claims, write the joint boundary, and add n/CIs to the F2 accuracy table.

**M7. The σ_eff/ICC analysis rests on three samples and a favourable window size, and its key structural assumption is asserted rather than evidenced.** §7b/Methods §5: (a) The between/within ICC decomposition uses **three samples** per anchor; the per-species ICCs range 0.42–0.86 (`data/sun_three_arm/sigma_icc.tsv`; two of 21 species below 0.55). "A median 74.8% of the dispersion [is] correctable" is defensible as stated, but the spread is material and should be reported beside the median, with a note that ICCs from n = 3 are themselves very noisy. (b) e_i is normalised by the species mean estimated from the same three samples; this induces a compositional negative correlation among e_i within a sample that biases both σ_eff and the ICC in unquantified directions — not discussed anywhere. (c) The "diluted by √99 window averaging to ~0.07–0.10" bound uses 99 anchors/window, the *maximum* of the `clamp(n/25, 25, 100)` window rule (Methods §1.3); sparse enzymes at 25 anchors/window get √25, i.e. a residual CV of ~0.14. The √99 figure is the best case presented as the case. (d) Methods §5 asserts "e_i is spatially uncorrelated along the genome" as a premise for window averaging; the supporting analysis exists in the repository (`data/sigma_structure_tests.*`, lagged autocorrelation within ±0.03) but is neither described nor cited in the manuscript. *Fix:* report the ICC distribution and its n=3 fragility; discuss the normalisation circularity; give the dilution bound per enzyme class; move the spatial-independence evidence into Methods.

**M8. The C5 confound control is real but does not isolate "fragmentation."** §7c reports ρ(qc_rate, n_contigs) = −0.41, ρ = −0.34 controlling mean coverage, p < 1e-4; the numbers verify against `data/c5_review/c5_coverage_control.tsv` (−0.409/−0.337, p = 2.5e-15), and the within-quartile strengthening in the highest-coverage quartile (−0.432) is a genuinely good control. However, the same file shows N50 (+0.358) and CheckM2 completeness (+0.353) retain partial correlations of essentially the same magnitude as n_contigs (−0.337) after the same coverage control. Controlling only mean coverage leaves the assembly-quality axis (N50, completeness, contig count are all collinear properties of the same MAGs) entangled: the data support "QC pass tracks assembly quality," of which fragmentation is one correlate, not an independent fragmentation effect. Also, qc_rate is a proportion over 9 samples with no binomial/overdispersion treatment, and the manuscript prints "p < 1e-4" where the file has p = 2.5e-15. *Fix:* control for completeness or N50 jointly (or report all three partial ρs side by side, as the data file already does), and reword the claim as assembly-quality tracking with fragmentation as the leading correlate.

**M9. Analysis provenance and multiplicity across the F1–F5 series.** The paper deserves real credit for its "Dropped claims" section and for reporting that Cochran's Q caught the authors' own double-counting bug — this is how self-skeptical methods reporting should look. But the F1–F5 experiments are clearly post-hoc (F1 overturned the "determinism" claim; §8 retracts the earlier "Pilea ~7× faster" claim; §5 notes the first panel sweep "said k = 2 beat the full panel" before the counting bug was found), and the manuscript never states which analyses were pre-specified versus exploratory. Claims like "r peaks at k = 8" and the GC-boundary sentences are maxima/thresholds selected over many correlated cells; without a provenance statement or CIs, the reader cannot discount selection. *Fix:* a short paragraph in Methods stating the pre-specified core (Zheng titration, simulation, fragmentation, Sun three-arm) versus the post-hoc F-series, plus bootstrap CIs (M3) so that selection-over-cells is at least visible.

## 4. Minor comments

- **m1.** §2.3 and Methods §4: "a threshold of 5 on k-mer coverage corresponds to ≈6.6× read coverage." The manuscript's own derivation (150 bp read → 120 31-mers) gives 5 × 150/120 = **6.25×**, not 6.6×. State the arithmetic or fix the number.

## 5. Top-3 missing experiments/analyses

1. **The symmetric-harness rerun of the F1 grid (A and E under identical flags), with CIs** — this decides whether the 0.5× headline (0.913 vs 0.568) and the source×estimator interaction are properties of the data or of an undocumented configuration (M1). Cost is modest: the grid exists, the reads exist, only the runs are missing.
2. **A complete accounting of the containment screen: true-positive loss and measured speedup at defined presence rates** — this decides whether the M4 screen can stand as "a measured fix" in §7c or must be demoted to future work (M2). All inputs already exist in the authors' C5 pipeline.
3. **Bootstrap CIs and a factorial test for the 2 × 2 interaction** — this decides whether "indistinguishable at matched density," "r peaks at k = 8," and the interaction claim survive quantified uncertainty rather than eyeballing (M3); it is pure re-analysis of existing tables.

## 6. Strongest and weakest part

The strongest part of the paper, from a methods standpoint, is §4 together with the evaluation-metrics section: crossing landmark source against estimator in a full 2 × 2 — with the arm-E construction correctly feeding Pilea's own sketch through the unmodified sk2bGrow estimator precisely to avoid the confound that "the surrounding machinery, not the fitting geometry, had been removed" — is exactly the attribution discipline this literature usually lacks, and pairing it with an explicit table of what each metric can and cannot detect (r invariant to affine transformation; L2 rewarding silence; recall and error read together) makes the paper's statistical reasoning visible rather than assumed. The honesty about its own failures (the double-count bug caught by its own Q, the retracted "determinism" and "~7× faster" claims) is rare and valuable. The weakest part is that this methodological care does not extend to uncertainty: the paper's single most important number — usable estimates at 0.5–1× — changes by 0.34 r between two harness configurations the authors themselves describe as asymmetric, no confidence interval appears anywhere in 12 tables, the slope switches its reference variable mid-Discussion onto a circular ground truth the Methods had just disclaimed, and a cost fix is reported with its false-positive benefit but not the true-positive cost recorded in the authors' own review notes. The paper is one symmetric rerun, one variance analysis, and one honest paragraph about the screen away from matching its own standards.

# Peer Review — Microbiome (Reviewer B: metagenomics biologist)

## 1. Summary

This manuscript presents sk2bGrow, a peak-to-trough ratio (PTR) estimator that replaces the FracMinHash sketch + sorted-rank regression of Pilea with motif-defined Type IIB restriction-enzyme (2bRAD) anchors of known genomic coordinate, fitted with a windowed coordinate-aware "V" and fused across sixteen enzyme strata. On a 16-media *E. coli* growth panel, subsampled to 0.5–10×, the method returns usable estimates (r = 0.981 against measured growth rate) at 1× where Pilea's shipped gates return nothing; a 2×2 attribution experiment shows the gain comes from the estimator × landmark-set interaction, not from landmark determinism, and that at density-matched landmarks the two landmark sources are indistinguishable at ≥1×. The paper adds a run-out negative control Pilea's benchmark omitted, a gate ablation showing Pilea's `--min-cove` gate is calibrated to where its own estimator breaks (~6.6× read coverage), fragmentation/scaffolding experiments, and real-metagenome sections: cross-method concordance on three fecal samples (n = 58–78 species×sample pairs each), a striking process finding that exact deduplication of 2bRAD libraries destroys the PTR signal, and a MAG-scale QC/cost study on a 9-sample environmental dataset. The writing is unusually honest — slope and RMSE accompany correlation throughout, and several tempting claims are reported as refuted rather than asserted.

## 2. Overall assessment

**Recommendation: major revision.**

- Novelty: **3/5** — the coordinate-aware estimator is a real conceptual advance over sorted-rank PTR, but the paper itself shows the landmark source is not the advance, which narrows the novelty to the estimator and the multi-strata design.
- Technical rigor: **4/5** — controlled 2×2 attribution, exact gate ablation, permutation nulls, and bias reported against a knowingly non-independent magnitude reference; docked for no-sequencing-error simulations and harness asymmetries the authors themselves flag.
- Biological/clinical significance: **2/5** — the method targets a biologically important quantity, but no biological finding is delivered on any real community; the real-data sections are concordance and QC engineering, and the motivating depth regime (1–2× per strain) is never actually exercised on a real metagenome.
- Clarity: **4/5** — prose is precise and self-critical; the draft carries placeholder citations and an internal assembly note that must go.
- Reproducibility: **4/5** — code, data tables, and figure regeneration are committed; several key results rest on ports/simulations rather than shipped CLI modes (acknowledged in-text).

Two-sentence justification: this is a methods paper executed with rare candour, and the negative control plus gate ablation are genuinely valuable to the field; but for a *Microbiome* readership the gap between the motivating use case (growth rates for low-coverage community members) and what is demonstrated on real communities is the central weakness, and the dedup recommendation — arguably the most citable finding for wet-lab users — currently lacks a mechanism and any evidence of generality beyond one library.

## 3. Major comments

**M1. The motivating depth regime is never demonstrated on a real metagenome.**
Location: Abstract ("usable PTR estimates at 1–2× sequencing depth"); Introduction ("metagenomic per-strain coverage is typically 1–2×, not isolate depth"); §2.1; §7.
All accuracy claims at 0.5–2× rest on reads subsampled from a single *E. coli* isolate run and on a simulation that adds **no sequencing error** (Methods §3.2 states this verbatim, noting it "favours the exact-match k-mer method, not ours" — asymmetric generosity is fine once, but it leaves the regime of interest untested). Meanwhile every real-metagenome experiment uses ultra-deep material: the Sun fecal samples are 125–135 Gb each (Methods §3.5), and C5 samples are 67–79 M read pairs (Methods §3.6) — in these data, the species that pass QC sit far above 1× per-strain coverage, so the band the paper exists for is never stress-tested against real error, real strain mixtures, and real GC/efficiency structure simultaneously. Why it matters: the paper's reason to exist is a use case — growth rates for the low-coverage majority of a community — that the evidence does not directly demonstrate. What would fix it, at modest cost: (i) re-run the Sun WGS subsampled to a realistic shallow cohort (e.g., 5–10 Gb/sample, where most species land at 1–2×) through both tools and report how many species retain usable estimates and at what concordance — a pure re-analysis of existing downloads; (ii) add one simulation arm with 1% substitution error to close the no-error caveat. Neither requires new sequencing.

**M2. No real community contributes any biological validation — concordance is not accuracy.**
Location: §7a ("We frame this as concordance validation (n = 3 samples), not a biological discovery"); §7c; Abstract (which nevertheless leads a microbiome journal readership toward growth-rate application).
On the fecal cohort the two methods agree with bias +0.22–+0.25 log₂ and r 0.34–0.51 (Table 10a, verified), but with no ground truth in real data, agreement between two estimators sharing the same PTR model assumptions (single strain per species, no relic DNA, chromosome-only) cannot distinguish both-right from both-wrong. The C5 section is titled "MAG-scale application" but yields no application result — no time-series analysis of the 9 samples, no ecological statement, only recall/QC/cost. I acknowledge the cost of a properly powered biological study with ground truth (it arguably does not exist for fecal PTR), and I credit the authors for explicitly bounding the claim. But two cheap analyses would materially strengthen this section: (i) rank estimated log₂PTR for fecal species with well-characterised physiology (e.g., obligately anaerobic slow growers vs facultative anaerobes) and report whether the ordering matches biological expectation — a re-analysis of existing output tables; (ii) in C5, test whether within-MAG cross-sample PTR consistency (already computed: median std 0.030 after QC, §7c) tracks any plausible temporal structure, or state plainly that the community is too sparse for biology. Absent this, §7 reads as engineering validation, and the paper should not gesture at "application."

**M3. The deduplication recommendation lacks a mechanism and any evidence of generality — this is the finding wet-lab users will cite.**
Location: §7b ("The process recommendation is therefore to *not* exactly deduplicate 2bRAD libraries before counting"); Discussion.
The finding rests on three samples, one enzyme (BcgI), one centre, one library-prep history (Methods §3.5 bounds this well). What is missing is the mechanism, and the obvious one is testable nearly for free: if PCR duplication probability tracks template copy number, then pre-dedup reads from origin-proximal anchors should be *more* duplicated than terminus-proximal ones, and exact dedup divides out exactly the gradient being measured. That signature — per-anchor duplication fraction regressed on circular distance from ori — is computable from the existing raw counts the authors already hold, and would (a) convert an empirical "don't" into a "why," (b) specify the boundary conditions of the recommendation (does it depend on cycle number, input mass, polymerase, enzyme choice?), and (c) tell users of *other* 2bRAD preps (2bRAD-M and its variants, different enzymes) whether the advice transfers. As written, a reader cannot tell whether this is a property of 2bRAD libraries or of this library. This is one regression on existing data — not a new experiment.

**M4. The PTR model's biological assumptions are listed but their expected magnitude in the target data is never quantified.**
Location: Discussion, "Limits of the biological model."
The four assumptions — single strain per species, relic DNA, multi-fork replication, plasmids — are correctly named, but a practising microbiomist cannot tell from this text whether fecal PTRs from sk2bGrow carry ~0.05 or ~0.5 log₂ of systematic bias from these sources, and some evidence already in hand bears on them. Specifically: (i) relic DNA flattens the gradient toward PTR = 1 and is a substantial, debated fraction of fecal DNA — the ZTP mixture of §1.3 already models absent anchors as a truncating component; the same machinery could report the estimated non-replicating fraction per species on the fecal data, turning a caveat into a number; (ii) strain mixing is stress-tested only in simulation against a deliberately adversarial *E. coli*/O157:H7/*Shigella* trio (§3.2) — no real strain-mixture or mock-community control exists anywhere in the paper; (iii) the two-slope multi-fork form "is exercised only weakly here" (Discussion), yet fast gut growers routinely sit at PTR > 2 where multi-fork kinks are real — the Zheng panel's top λ = 1.72 h⁻¹ barely enters that regime, so the shipped method's behaviour where biologists most need it is untested; (iv) plasmids are counted as chromosomal, which for plasmid-rich gut taxa is a concrete bias direction, not a footnote. I do not demand new datasets; I ask that the paper quantify what its existing data can quantify (the relic fraction; one multi-fork simulation cell at planted log₂PTR ∈ [2, 3]) and state expected bias directions per assumption in units a user can reason with.

**M5. The instrument boundary (GC ≳ 30% at ≳ 1×) rests on a genome-size confound and cells with n as low as 5.**
Location: §5 ("the two failing genomes are 0.65 Mb *Buchnera*, so the boundary is confounded with genome size and is flagged, not resolved"); Table 9 (same row).

**M6. The abstract borrows a cross-study number the authors' own discipline forbids.**

**M7. The C5 dataset is described three biologically incompatible ways; at least one must be wrong.**

# Peer Review — Microbiome (Reviewer 丙 / adversarial consistency & systems reviewer)

## 1. Summary

This paper presents sk2bGrow, a peak-to-trough ratio (PTR) estimator that counts Type IIB restriction-enzyme (2bRAD) anchors — motif-defined loci with a priori known genome coordinates — and fits the replication gradient with a coordinate-aware windowed V-fit, fusing per-enzyme estimates across a 16-enzyme panel. Benchmarked on the Zheng *E. coli* growth-rate panel titrated to 0.5–10×, it returns usable estimates at 1–2× where Pilea's shipped coverage gates report nothing (r = 0.981 vs measured growth rate at 1×, n = 16). A controlled 2 × 2 attribution (landmark source × estimator) is used to argue the advance is the *interaction* of the coordinate-aware estimator with the multi-enzyme panel, not either factor alone. The paper adds a negative run-out control Pilea excluded, a laptop-scale multi-strain simulation, fragmentation/scaffolding experiments, three fecal metagenomes of cross-method concordance plus a process-level finding that exact dedup destroys the 2bRAD PTR signal, a MAG-scale application (522 MAGs × 9 samples), and a cost analysis concluding the tool is 89.5–240.8× slower than Pilea at MAG-panel scale, narrowed to ~10–25× by mismatch-1 counting plus a containment screen. The manuscript is unusually self-critical — bias caveats, dropped claims, and a cannot-claim list are stated explicitly — which I credit. However, my job is consistency, and I found places where the manuscript's own numbers, its own internal review documents, and its own claim boundaries do not line up.

## 2. Overall assessment

**Recommendation: major revision.**

Scores (1–5): novelty **4**, technical rigor **3**, biological/clinical significance **2**, clarity **3**, reproducibility **3**.

The idea — coordinate-aware regression on motif-defined anchors, with a crossed attribution design isolating estimator from landmark source — is genuinely new and the internal verification culture (gate ablations, permutation nulls, QC self-audits) is better than most papers I review; I verified over twenty of the manuscript's numbers against the repo's tables and the large majority are accurate. But the revision must fix real internal contradictions I document below (a Discussion slope that contradicts Table 2 under the paper's own metric definition; enzyme-ranking threshold claims that are false against the paper's own Table 6), one headline cost-reduction figure that is unverifiable from the repository and contradicted by the only direct measurement of that mechanism in the repo, and a title/abstract emphasis on "deterministic anchors" that the paper itself has measured to be irrelevant.

## 3. Major comments

**M1. The "21.1× at 90% selection stringency" containment-screen speedup is unverifiable from the repository, and the repository's own review documents that the shipped screen is currently neither fast nor correct in the regime it was directly tested.**
Location: §7c, "the M4 containment pre-screen clears the absent-genome false-positive floor … returning 21.1× at 90% selection stringency. The flip is therefore real but fixable, and the fixes are measured rather than projected"; echoed in the Abstract ("narrowed to ~10–25× by mismatch-1 counting plus a containment screen").
What is wrong: I searched the repository (`data/c5_review/`, `data/f5_index_cost/`, all tables) for any measurement supporting "21.1×" or "90% selection stringency" and found none — **unverifiable from repo**. Worse, the repo's own review of the screen experiment (`data/c5_review/REVIEW.md` §4d) records that on the only directly timed comparison (identical 1M read-pair subset, 522-MAG database with `--screen`): wall-clock was **0.99×** (lookup −5.4%) — i.e., *no speedup* — and the screened run silently dropped **65 of 522 real MAGs** (457/522 retained), concentrated in low-abundance genomes, because of the `--screen-min-frac` default. The review's own verdict on the M4 screen in the current implementation is "既不快也不对" (neither fast nor correct) for the PTR use case, with the speedup judgment explicitly deferred to the dilution experiment — which measured only the false-positive census (4,048/4,700 synthetic genomes with nonzero counts unrestricted, 0 with screen; that part checks out), **not** any 21.1× speedup. Why it matters: the Abstract's closing sentence attributes the cost fix to "mismatch-1 counting plus a containment screen," and §7c declares the fixes "measured rather than projected." In fact: mm=1's 63× lookup speedup was measured only on a 1M-pair subset (wall 8.6×, `REVIEW.md` §4f) and the ~10–25× full-sample figure is an extrapolation of that subset speedup onto 16–27 h runs; and the screen's contribution to the narrowing has no documented measurement at all, while its documented behavior (dropping the lowest-coverage real genomes — exactly the population this paper's low-coverage thesis concerns) is nowhere disclosed in the manuscript. What would fix it: either produce the measurement (run the screened and mm=1 arms end-to-end on one full C5 sample — at the extrapolated 1.9–3.1 h this is feasible) or rewrite the Abstract/§7c to attribute the narrowing to mm=1 alone, label the 10–25× as subset-extrapolated, and disclose the screen's low-abundance genome-loss behavior and zero measured speedup at realistic presence rates.

**M2. The Discussion's "slope 1.03 at 5× and 1.07 at 10×" contradicts Table 2 under the paper's own slope definition, and the "over-estimation at depth" inference rests on the reference the Methods tell readers not to trust for validation.**
Location: Discussion, "The compression and the mild over-estimation at depth (slope 1.03 at 5× and 1.07 at 10×, against 0.62 at 0.5× and 0.78 at 1×)". §5 defines slope as "OLS slope of *ŷ* on λ"; Table 2 (and its generator `figures/make_tables.py:142`, `np.polyfit(growth_rate, log2ptr, 1)`) implements exactly that. The λ-slopes for sk2bGrow at 5×/10× are **0.920/0.951** (`tables/table2_ecoli_accuracy.tsv`), not 1.03/1.07. I reproduced the 1.03/1.07 by regressing on the *predicted* log₂PTR instead (`data/results_raw.tsv`: slope vs predicted = 1.030/1.074 at 5×/10×) — so the sentence silently mixes two different slope definitions, and the 0.62/0.78 half of the sentence uses the λ-based numbers. The ≥1 values are only obtainable against the predicted reference, which Methods §3.1 explicitly flags as "not fully independent of sequencing" (C derived in part by marker-frequency analysis of the same reads) and instructs should be read as "a consistency check on magnitude, not an independent validation." Why it matters: "the estimates mildly over-estimate at depth" (Abstract) is thereby supported only by the non-independent reference (bias +0.031 at 10× is likewise computed against predicted log₂PTR). This may well be real, but as written the paper's own metric table and its Discussion disagree, and the abstract-level caveat inherits the non-independence the Methods warn about. What would fix it: recompute and state which reference each slope uses; either report the λ-slopes throughout (0.92/0.95 at depth) or add one sentence acknowledging that the >1 slopes are against the λC-predicted reference and therefore a magnitude-consistency statement, not independent evidence of over-estimation.

**M3. The enzyme-ranking threshold claims in §5 are factually wrong against the paper's own Table 6.**
Location: §5, "the top six enzymes are the six with > 1,900 anchors, and the bottom four, with < 450 anchors each, are near-zero or negative at 0.5×."
Against `tables/table6_enzyme_ranking.tsv`: the top six by rank are CjePI (7,616 anchors), CjeI (8,797), AlfI (**1,989**), Hin4I (5,330), HaeIV (6,435), BcgI (2,872). BslFI has **2,501** anchors (> 1,900) yet ranks 9th — so "the top six are the six with > 1,900 anchors" is false. The bottom four by rank are FalI (**687**), AloI (441), BplI (379), PsrI (419) — two of the four have > 450 anchors, and the four enzymes actually below 450 anchors include PpiI (336), which ranks 11th, not bottom-four. (Table 6's own caption repeats the error with a "> 2,500" threshold, contradicted by rank-3 AlfI at 1,989.) Why it matters: §5's panel-composition recommendation ("4–8 enzymes, not 16") is one of the paper's practical deliverables, and it is argued from this yield-dominance pattern; the true pattern ("ranking tracks anchor yield with exceptions" — BslFI and BslFI-type exceptions, and per-enzyme r@1× varying 0.91–0.96 among mid-yield enzymes) is actually *more* interesting and would strengthen the argument, since rank 3 (AlfI, 1,989 anchors, r@1× = 0.914) shows yield is not destiny. What would fix it: restate as a correlation/regression of rank on log anchor yield with the named exceptions, and correct Table 6's caption.

**M4. The flagship 0.5× accuracy is harness-dependent to the tune of 0.35 r, and the 0.5× ordering that motivates the paper's central attribution claim flips between harnesses.**
Location: §2.1/Table 2 report anchors+V-fit r = 0.913 at 0.5×; §4 reports the same arm at 0.5× as r = 0.568 in the F1 harness and states "cross-harness comparisons are invalid — the A-baseline measured in the F1 harness (r 0.568 at 0.5×) differs from the committed arm-A number in the C8 harness (0.913)… a symmetric rerun remains a check, not a result."
I verified both numbers (`data/f1_sketch/F1_results.tsv`: A_k16 0.5× r = 0.568, n = 16; Table 2: 0.913) and I accept the authors' transparency here. But as a consistency matter the consequences are under-appreciated in the text: (i) the Abstract/§2 headline "usable at 0.5×" number (0.913) and the §4 mechanism story are measured in two harnesses whose results for the *identical arm on identical reads* differ by 0.345 r, and the paper does not know why (the stated difference is parity flags `--windows` vs not); (ii) in the F1 harness at 0.5×, the sketch+V-fit arm *beats* the anchor+V-fit arm on surviving cells (0.818 vs 0.568) — so "the coordinate-aware estimator, not the landmark set, carries the low-coverage result" (§4 opening sentence) is harness-dependent at exactly the depth the paper is about. The survivor-selection argument (sketch loses 6/17 cells) is fair but partially concedes the point. Why it matters: the paper's single most important causal claim — "the advance is the interaction" — rests on 0.5× cells whose ordering is not stable across the two harnesses the authors themselves ran. What would fix it: the symmetric rerun the authors already flag as "a check" must be promoted from a footnote to a result before publication. It is cheap (existing HPC pipeline, same reads, one parity flag), and it decides whether the 0.5× attribution survives. If the ordering is harness-artifact, the honest claim shrinks to "the panel maintains yield at 0.5× via fusion redundancy" (which is harness-robust) and the interaction claim is restricted to ≥1×.

**M5. Title and framing still overstate the deterministic-anchor property that the paper's own attribution experiment retired.**
Location: Title, "Coordinate-aware peak-to-trough estimation from deterministic 2bRAD anchors"; Introduction, "their coordinates are known before any read is seen and are identical in every sample… a sketch with a different geometry"; Discussion/Table 9, where the row "reference determinism" states "the determinism claim was dropped" and "accuracy at matched density, ≥1×: identical" for both landmark sources.
The paper's own F1 result is that at matched landmark density the deterministic motif anchors are *indistinguishable* from a random FracMinHash sketch at ≥1× (verified: `data/f1_sketch/F1_results.tsv`, 9,645 vs 9,422 landmarks/Mb), that determinism per se was checked and dropped as a distinguishing property (Discussion, "Dropped claims"), and that what survives is (a) the coordinate-aware estimator and (b) multi-strata fusion redundancy at 0.5×. A title built on "deterministic 2bRAD anchors" foregrounds precisely the property the data deprioritize; nothing in the title or abstract mentions the panel/interaction, which the paper says is the actual advance. Why it matters: titles are claims; readers (and citation amplifiers) will read this as "deterministic anchors beat sketches," which the paper's own Table 9 refutes row by row. What would fix it: either retitle to foreground what is supported (e.g., "…from a coordinate-aware multi-enzyme 2bRAD panel" / "Coordinate-aware PTR estimation and the enzyme-panel interaction") or add an explicit sentence in the Introduction stating that "deterministic" refers to enumerability/coordinate-knowability, not to any measured accuracy advantage over a deterministic hash sketch. The authors' own outline concedes "the data does not support a sketch-first framing" — the current title is half sketch-first.

**M6. The GTDB index projections (232/184/124 GB) are stated without their premises, and the "~39 B/landmark either way" cost-parity claim is a counterfactual, not a measurement.**
Location: §8, "Index cost is a wash between modes at matched density (~39 B per landmark either way, equal build speed)… Projected to GTDB species representatives, a k16 enzyme index is ~232 GB, k8 ~184 GB, and a half-density FracMinHash index (scale 200, 0.53× landmarks) ~124 GB."
The premises exist only in the internal review (`data/f5_index_cost/REVIEW.md` §4): 136,646 GTDB species representatives × ~4.6 Mb ≈ 629 Gbp × measured 9,519 anchors/Mb (300-genome sample) × 38.8 B/anchor. None of — representative count, mean genome size, measured density, or the bytes-per-anchor constant — appears in the manuscript, so the projections are unverifiable for a reader (I could verify them only by reading the internal review). Further, the measured FMH arm's *actual* current format is **23.4 B/locus** (`F5_index_cost.tsv`, sketch rows; REVIEW §1), i.e., ~40% smaller per landmark than the enzyme format; "~39 B either way" is true only of a hypothetical FMH re-engineering into the enzyme index format (Table 9 hedges this; §8 does not). "Equal build speed" is loosely supported (3.0 s vs 1–2 s per genome). Why it matters: scale/cost is one of the three things a *Microbiome* reader will take from §8, and the parity claim as written inflates a hypothetical into a measurement. What would fix it: state the projection constants in Methods §6.5 (one sentence); rephrase §8 to "in a common index format the per-landmark disk cost is isomorphic (~39 B); the current FMH prototype stores 23.4 B/locus but lacks mismatch-verification tags and a merged index, so the parity is by construction, not measurement."

**M7. The C5 "QC works on real data" claim is based on fusion outputs generated before the QC columns being validated existed; the repo's own audit found the QC passes untested and degenerate cells.**
Location: §7c, "The enzyme-consistency QC demonstrably filters fragmented references on real data: QC pass rate anti-correlates with MAG contig count (ρ = −0.41; ρ = −0.34 controlling mean coverage, p < 1e-4)…"; the ρ values check out against `data/c5_review/c5_qc_x_quality_with_cov.tsv`. But `data/c5_review/REVIEW.md` §6b (qc_audit over 9 samples × 522 MAGs) records: (i) the C5 fusion tables "生成于 a56197d 之前，无 consistency_checked/min_r2 列" — the outputs scored here were produced by an older build lacking the very consistency/min-r² fields the QC claim is about; (ii) 27 cells (0.57%) pass with k = 1 enzyme (Cochran's Q never run, recorded as consistent) and 33 fused rows (0.70%) carry n_enzymes = 1; (iii) 1.9% of accepted fits have r² < 0 — "QC 不看拟合质量在真实数据上同样成立" (the QC does not look at fit quality holds on real data too). Why it matters: §6.3 and the Discussion build an honest two-sided story about the QC (it caught the double-count; it is blind to destroyed coordinates), and §7c upgrades it to "works on real data." The audit shows the C5 evaluation predates the current QC and that a small but nonzero fraction of "QC-passed" rows passed no consistency test at all. Magnitudes are small (≤2%), so this is unlikely to change conclusions — but the manuscript's entire ethos is pairing every yield number with its caveat, and this one is missing. What would fix it: re-fuse the C5 tables with the current stats layer (the review notes this requires no recounting), add one sentence reporting the k = 1 / r² < 0 acceptance rates as QC boundary conditions, and date-stamp the QC version used for §7c.

## 5. Top-3 missing experiments/analyses

1. **End-to-end cost validation of the mm=1 (and screened) configuration on one full C5 sample** — the abstract's "narrowed to ~10–25×" is currently a subset-extrapolation and the screen's 21.1× is unverifiable; one 2–3 h run converts the paper's least-supported quantitative claim into a measured one, and forces disclosure of the screen's low-abundance genome losses at realistic presence rates.
2. **A harness-symmetric rerun of the F1 0.5× cells (arms A and E under identical parity flags)** — decides whether the 0.5× attribution ("only the combination works") and the §4 headline are real or a harness artifact; cheap (existing pipeline, same reads), and currently conceded as "a check, not a result."
3. **A reference-divergence sensitivity benchmark on real assemblies** (even a 500-genome subset of the planned 45,529-*Escherichia* sweep, crossing ANI 95–99.9% × N50) — the only divergence evidence now is synthetic (0.1% SNP mutants; planted-error F3), and §6 shows Pilea wins on fragmented references at 1×, so the MAG-use claim currently leans on scaffolding validated on n = 2–3 genomes; this decides whether "scaffold anything not closed" is sufficient at strain-level divergence. (I acknowledge the full sweep is cluster-scale; a thinned version is not.)

## 6. Strongest and weakest part

The strongest part is the attribution architecture and the intellectual honesty around it: the 2 × 2 crossing of landmark source and estimator (with arm E honestly built by re-feeding Pilea's own sketch through the unmodified estimator), the refusal to claim determinism once density-matching refuted it, the gate ablation showing `--min-cove 5` alone suppresses 68/68 *E. coli* estimates at ≈6.6× effective depth while also showing the gate is *partly right* (fully degenerate gates-off output at 0.5×), and the QC narrative in which Cochran's Q caught the authors' own double-counting bug. This is how methods comparisons should be done, and the numbers behind it largely check out — I verified the gate ablation, simulation aggregates, panel sweep, fragmentation table, run-out control, fecal concordance coefficients, dedup sensitivity, σ_eff/ICC, C5 recall ranges, per-depth timing profile, and F1/F3/F4/F5 figures against the repo and found them accurate (with the exceptions listed above). The weakest part is the scale/cost story in §7c–§8: the one place where the paper's claims outrun its own evidence is precisely the systems territory where I would hold it to the highest standard — an unverifiable 21.1× screen speedup contradicted by the repo's own direct measurement of that screen, an extrapolated 10–25× presented as "measured rather than projected," an undisclosed genome-dropping failure mode of the shipped screen, and index-size projections whose premises never made it out of the internal review documents. Because the rest of the paper is so carefully hedged, these overreaches stand out all the more; all are fixable without new science — one full-sample run, one symmetric rerun, and honest relabeling of what is measured versus projected would bring §7c–§8 up to the standard the rest of the manuscript already meets.
